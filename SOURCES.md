# Sources of the place packs

Generated from sources.json by tools/write_sources_md.py. Licences checked in November 2026; check them again when a source changes its terms. Part: "core" packs (smaller) or "extra" packs.

Personal data: the business sources (inspections, licences, registrations, directories) keep only a trade name, a class, a position and dates, never scores, violations or owners. Business types that are often run from a home (contractors, consultants, home caterers, childminders, short-term rentals, individual licences) are left out by each source's class map, only trade names are used where the source separates them from the owner's name, and licensee names that look like a person's are left out. The notes below say how for each source.

US businesses: there is no federal database of businesses with positions (the counterpart of Statistics Canada's Open Database of Businesses). The US business backbone is Overture Maps places, All The Places (chains' own store locators), OpenStreetMap and the city and state licence and inspection lists below; the IRS exempt-organization file adds non-profits. Aggregated statistics (County Business Patterns and the like) have no place names and are not used.

## Notes on individual sources

- National Address Database (USDOT): Downloaded from USDOT's data.transportation.gov (dataset fc2s-wawr, the "TXT.zip" file, about 8.5 GB): its address is read from the dataset's metadata at each build, and the old nationaladdressdata S3 bucket (now refusing downloads) is only a fallback. The download is kept in the Actions cache and continues where it stopped after a dropped connection. USDOT marks the data of HI, MI, MN, NH, OR, SC and SD, and parts of CO, IL, KS, MD and ND, as possibly not public domain; those states are left out until their terms are checked. State laws forbid using it as a mailing list; the packs are for naming places in pictures.
- OpenAddresses: Only sources whose licence is public domain, CC0, CC BY, ODC-By or OGL-type are used (allowed_licences); share-alike and non-commercial sources are left out. Mainly for the states the NAD leaves out. Put the source's CSV and its JSON (with the licence) in inputs/openaddresses/<region id>/.
- US Census Bureau TIGER/Line point landmarks: Named point landmarks typed by their MTFCC code: summits, schools, churches, hospitals, cemeteries, airports, post offices, monuments, lookout towers and more. Homes (apartment buildings, trailer courts, dormitories, nursing homes), shelters, survey marks and road features are left out.
- US Census Bureau TIGER/Line area landmarks: Named area landmarks typed by their MTFCC code: parks of every level, national forests, cemeteries, golf courses, airports, colleges, shopping centres, campgrounds, zoos and more; homes and dormitories are left out.
- US Census Bureau TIGER/Line military installations: Military installations, one national file each year (only those touching the region are read); with the seed years, a base closed since 2011 keeps its last-seen year.
- Overture Maps Foundation places: Overture renamed its category columns in 2026 ("categories" became "taxonomy" and "basic_category"): the build reads the release's columns first and uses taxonomy.primary, else basic_category, else the old categories.primary. Places whose operating_status is "permanently_closed" are left out of the month's data, so their earlier record stays, closed at the last build that listed them open.
- Foursquare Open Source Places: Foursquare's files are not sorted by place, so the prepare job reads them once for every covered country and each region takes its box from that local copy (one download instead of one per region).
- USGS Geographic Names Information System: Cemeteries have their own class (cemetery). Military features (forts, camps, bases, many of them gone) are kept as landmarks; GNIS marks a feature that no longer exists with "(historical)" in its name, and that name is kept as it is, so a lookup says "Fort Example (historical)".
- NCES public school locations: The EDGE geocode download holds a shapefile (no CSV): read as points, split per state by its box.
- IMLS Public Libraries Survey outlets: The newest outlet file is found on IMLS's survey page each build (no hand step). Central and branch libraries only; bookmobiles and books-by-mail have no fixed place.
- National Register of Historic Places: Read from the National Park Service's Cultural Resources GIS service (no key). The listing date is not the building's age: a picture older than the listing still gets the name, said "no older record".
- USGS Protected Areas Database of the United States (PAD-US): Read from USGS's PAD-US 4.1 service (no key). Every state park, state recreation area, state historic or cultural area, state wilderness and state forest, of any size; other named areas of at least 10 acres that are owned or designated (no easements, which are often private land) and open or restricted to the public. Outlines simplified to about 50 m. Vermont gets 61 state parks and Ohio 89.
- FAA airports (NASR): Only public-use facilities that are open: private strips and heliports, often at homes, are left out.
- BTS National Transportation Atlas: Amtrak stations: Train stations only (Amtrak's bus stops are left out).
- Wikidata (every item with coordinates): Every Wikidata item with coordinates in the region, asked tile by tile (a tile that times out or fills 50,000 rows is split into four), typed by the English label of its class ("covered bridge", "brewpub"). Left out by placepacks/wikidata_exclude.json: people, families, trees, survey points, list and disambiguation pages, astronomical objects, taxa, electoral districts, graves and benches; private homes unless they have a heritage designation (P1435). Items that only copy a GNIS record (a GNIS id and no dates or designation) are left to GNIS. Only the id, English label, coordinates, class and dates are used, never Wikipedia text.
- Statistics Canada open databases of educational and cultural and art facilities: Downloaded from Statistics Canada's Linkable Open Data Environment (ODEF 2.1, ODCAF 1.0) at each build (no hand step); split by province. Healthcare facilities come from ODHF version 2 (statcan-odhf2).
- New York City restaurant inspections (DOHMH): New establishments not yet inspected carry the date 1900-01-01 and are left out until their first inspection.
- New York State food service establishment inspections (NYSDOH): The operators' personal names in the source are not read.
- Delaware food establishment inspections: The dataset has no permit number: records are keyed by name and street address.
- Montgomery County, Maryland food inspections: The owner column of the source is not read.
- New York State Liquor Authority licences (active and inactive): Only premises open to the public (restaurants, bars, shops, hotels, wineries and breweries); additional bars, wholesalers, importers, direct shippers and temporary permits are left out. The trade name is used, or the licensee's name when there is none; a licensee name that looks like a person's ("Surname, Given") is left out.
- Colorado liquor licences (current and recently expired): Only premises open to the public; permits, wholesalers, importers and direct shippers are left out. A licensee name that looks like a person's is left out.
- San Francisco registered business locations: Only storefront business types (restaurants, bars, shops, hotels and motels (not short-term rentals or bed and breakfasts), petrol stations, garages, salons, laundries, banks, cinemas, gyms; by NAICS code) and only the business's trade name (dba_name): offices, contractors and home services, which are often registered at a home address, and owners' names are left out.
- Los Angeles businesses (active and closed): Only storefront business types (by NAICS code) and only the trade name (dba_name), as for San Francisco; the placeholder position 34.0, -118.0 is left out.
- New York City consumer licences of storefront businesses (DCWP): Only premises licences of storefront businesses, and only their trade names: home improvement contractors, individual licences (vendors, drivers, guides) and licences without a trade name, which would show a person's name or home address, are left out.
- FDIC bank branch locations: The API needs no key ("users are currently not required to register"); it does not answer HEAD requests, so its version is the build month.
- Food Standards Agency food hygiene ratings (UK): Read from the FSA's open data files (one XML file per local authority, no key or login). Only restaurants, pubs, takeaways, shops and hotels: mobile caterers, home caterers, childminders and care premises, which may be private homes, are left out. No ratings or scores are kept.
- Sands & McDougall business directories of Victoria, 1896-1974: About 550,000 listings from the Victoria Unearthed project, geocoded by the Victorian government; read page by page from its WFS service (no login). Some listings are tradespeople's names as printed in the directories (1896 to 1974).
- GeoNames: A fallback for the countries outside North America (the US and Canada have GNIS and the Canadian names): lakes, bays, mountains, islands, parks, quarters of towns, churches, castles, monuments, museums, schools, hospitals, stations and airports, from the per-country files (no login).
- GeoNames (United States and Canada: kinds of places GNIS and the CGNDB lack): Only the kinds of places that GNIS and the Canadian Geographical Names Database do not hold: hotels, resorts, malls, casinos, restaurants, markets, amusement parks, zoos, gardens, observatories, museums, theatres, stadiums, golf courses, racetracks, metro stations, ports and named areas and regions. Localities, summits, streams, lakes and the rest are left to GNIS and the CGNDB, which GeoNames copied them from, so nothing is doubled.
- FHWA National Bridge Inventory (historic and long bridges): The inventory has no bridge names, so each is named by what it carries and crosses ("Middle Rd bridge over Hudson Brook"). Only bridges on or eligible for the National Register and bridges at least 150 m long are kept (about 270 in Vermont and 1,100 in Ohio, of 4,500 and 32,000).
- USACE National Inventory of Dams (named dams): Named dams only (about 770 in Vermont and 1,700 in Ohio). Owners' names are not read.
- National Park Service points of interest: Read from the NPS public datasets service (no API key). Each name carries its park ("Visitor Center (Saint-Gaudens National Historical Park)"), since many are generic. Only named places a photo can show: trailheads, campgrounds, visitor centres, overlooks, peaks, waterfalls, historic buildings and sites, monuments; parking lots, restrooms, mile markers and benches are left out.
- US Forest Service recreation sites: Read from the Forest Service's EDW map service (no RIDB key). Existing developed sites only; recreation residences (private cabins), dispersed camping spots and single camp units are left out.
- Atlas of Historical County Boundaries (Newberry Library): Non-county areas (unorganised land) are left out. Lookups give the county as it was on the picture's date. The national file (generalised to about 100 m) has shifted offsets inside its .zip, which the build repairs. The Newberry's terms: "available ... for any lawful purpose, commercial or non-commercial, without licensing or permission fees"; the older Creative Commons note inside the file is superseded.
- Washington, DC basic business licences (storefronts): Only premises in DC of storefront activities, under their trade names: rentals (one- and two-family, short-term and vacation rentals, rooming houses), contractors, salespeople and owners' and agents' names are left out.
- USCG Light List: lighthouses and major lights: Read from the Navigation Center's Light List volumes I-VII: the XML files when the Light List page offers them, else the text PDFs, read with pypdf (no OCR: the volumes are text; a scanned volume is reported and left out). Only lights named "... Light" whose structure is a tower, house or lighthouse are kept; buoys, daybeacons, range lights and lights on skeleton towers, poles, posts, dolphins or spindles are left out. Positions are converted from the list's degrees, minutes and seconds. navcen.uscg.gov refused requests from the development sandbox (403); the build tries from GitHub Actions and reports "skipped (could not be downloaded: ...)" when it is refused there too.
- USACE Formerly Used Defense Sites (FUDS): About 10,000 properties nationally, keyed by the FUDS property number; only those eligible for the program (the DoD did own or use them). The current owners' names are not read. The dates of DoD use appear only in free-text descriptions, which are not parsed; Wikidata gives dates for the better-known forts and bases.
- VA national cemeteries: Cemetery sites only (about 150): never burial or gravesite records.
- OpenHistoricalMap: Read from OHM's Overpass API, one query per region (OHM's planet files are archived and cannot be downloaded). Named nodes and ways with a start or end date, as points (a way at its centre); lines (streets, railway lines, rivers), boundaries and relations are left out. OHM's data is CC0 except features carrying their own licence tag, which are left out. About 1,900 features in Vermont's box and 34,000 in Ohio's.
- Statistics Canada Open Database of Recreational and Sport Facilities: Named facilities only (parks, arenas, pools, fields, playgrounds); paths and trails, which are lines, are left out. About 114,000 records in Ontario and 22,000 in British Columbia before that.
- Aboriginal Lands of Canada Legislative Boundaries (Canada Lands Survey System): About 3,400 Indian reserves, settlements and other Aboriginal lands, as areas (class region: named areas, not towns).
- Toronto DineSafe food premises inspections: Only the establishment's name, position and inspection dates are read (no infractions or fines).
- Boston food establishment licences and inspections: The inspection file is grouped by the portal itself to one row per licence and name; the owners' names and the violations are never read.
- IMLS Museum Universe Data File: About 15,000 museums, zoos, aquariums, botanical gardens, science and nature centers (the type is the IMLS discipline: art museum, history museum, zoo...). Historical societies (about 14,800) are left out: many are run from a member's home or a post office box.
- EPA BEACON beach monitoring locations: Each beach's monitoring locations, named by the beach ("- Location 1" removed); a beach with several locations has a point at each.
- US Forest Service trails: Named trails as lines (about 86,000 segments nationally), simplified to about 10 m: a photo on one is "on the X Trail" when nothing nearer names it.
- National Park Service trails: Named, publicly displayed park trails as lines (about 31,000 segments nationally).
- National Wild and Scenic Rivers: 249 designated rivers as lines: a photo beside one is "beside the X River" when nothing nearer names it.
- IRS Exempt Organizations Business Master File: Every exempt organization of the state (churches, schools, museums, clubs, foundations...), typed by its NTEE code. Addresses are located offline against the state's NAD address points; post office boxes cannot be located and are left out; an address the NAD marks residential (often an officer's home) is put at the middle of the street's points in its ZIP, not at the house. The states the NAD leaves out have no address points, so their organizations are not located.
- GSA Inventory of Owned and Leased Properties (federal buildings): Active federally owned and leased buildings (courthouses, federal buildings, offices) with their own names. The contact people's names and e-mail addresses in the layer are never read.
- NPS American Battlefield Protection Program battlefields: The core area of each surveyed battlefield (the fighting itself, not the wider study area), as areas: "Shepherdstown Battlefield".
- USGS GNIS variant names and historical features: Variant names (former names, other spellings: "Namagonac" for Seymour Lake) have their own class, variant: a name search finds the place by an old name, but a lookup never says a variant as the place's name. Offices of individuals (insurance and estate agents, financial advisers, lawyers) and names that are a person's are left out. The brand and its Wikidata id are kept beside each location (data only). Past runs back to 2017 are archived and could seed earlier dates (one a quarter: 55 MB in 2020, 2.4 GB in 2026).
- New York State Thruway travel plazas: Service plazas on the Thruway.
- New York State Thruway park-and-ride lots: Commuter lots along the Thruway.
- New York State farmers' markets and farm stands: Farmers' markets and farm stands. Only the market's name and point are used (never the contact person or phone).
- PennDOT park-and-ride lots: PennDOT's park-and-ride lots, named by their place ("Erie Area Lincoln Avenue at Interstate 79").
- US Fish and Wildlife Service national fish hatcheries: National fish hatcheries and fish technology centres, as the land the Service owns (FWS National Realty Tracts). Easements (private land) are left out. Refuges come from PAD-US.

- MSHA mines: About 90,000 coal and metal/non-metal mines, quarries and sand and gravel pits; an abandoned mine closes at its status date.
- Statistics Canada Open Database of Healthcare Facilities v2 (2026): About 22,000 hospitals, clinics, nursing and residential care facilities (version 2, September 2026; it replaces version 1.1 in the facilities entry). Positions are in Statistics Canada's Lambert projection and are converted.
- USGS Mineral Resources Data System (producers): Named mines that produced or once produced (development status Producer or Past Producer; prospects and occurrences are left out), with their first and last production years where known. MRDS is no longer updated.
- HRSA health center service delivery sites: Active community health centre sites with the date each was added. Mobile vans and sites in shelters, domestic-violence settings, homes and correctional facilities are left out.
- National Register of Historic Places districts (boundaries): About 14,600 historic district boundaries, each from its listing date (the points layer already gives every listed property).
- US Census Bureau TIGER/Line unified school districts: Unified school districts as named areas (class region).
- US Census Bureau TIGER/Line elementary school districts: Elementary school districts as named areas (class region).
- US Census Bureau TIGER/Line secondary school districts: Secondary school districts as named areas (class region).
- New York City individual landmarks: 1,532 individual landmarks, each from its designation date.
- US Wind Turbine Database (projects): Wind projects, one point per project (its first turbine), each from the year it came online.
- New Orleans HDLC landmarks: 350 designated landmarks.
- Kansas City food permits: About 3,700 permitted restaurants, bars, bakeries and markets; mobile units, pushcarts and catering permits are left out.
- Montgomery County (Maryland) alcohol beverage licensees: About 1,100 licensed restaurants, bars and stores by trade name.
- Baton Rouge historic markers: 279 historic markers (the local and national landmark layers are polygons only and are not read).
- FAA special use airspace: 1,545 military operations areas and restricted, warning, alert and prohibited areas, for pictures taken from the air. Data only: the class airspace is not an area class of the lookup, so it is never spoken.
- Directory of Federal Real Property (Canada): Named federal properties: wharves, lighthouses, RCMP detachments, border crossings, research stations, offices. Housing and residential properties and names that are only codes are left out.
- Federal Contaminated Sites Inventory (Canada): Federal sites by name, many of them former military, radar and DEW Line stations, airfields and mines.
- FHWA National Scenic Byways and All-American Roads: 230 designated byways as named lines (a picture on one is "on the X"). The service refuses box queries, so all 230 are asked for and each region keeps those that touch it.
- NOAA tide and water-level stations: About 3,500 tide prediction and water-level stations named for their place ("Nonopapa, Niihau Island").
- NOAA National Marine Sanctuaries: The sanctuaries and marine national monuments (47 areas) as named water areas.
- BIA Land Area Representation: 335 trust and reservation land areas, including trust lands that TIGER's American Indian areas lack (class region).
- Ontario fishing access points: About 3,700 boat launches and shore fishing access points.
- British Columbia ferry terminals: BC Ferries and inland ferry terminals.
- New Brunswick public schools: 294 public schools, each from the year it was built.
- NRCan principal producing mines (Map 900A): Producing metal, non-metal and coal mines and oil sands operations.
- NRCan oil and gas fields (Map 900A): 275 named oil and gas fields.
- New York State historic sites: 41 State Historic Sites.
- New York State park facilities: 254 state parks and facilities.
- New York State boat launch sites: 298 boat launches by waterbody.
- NTAD spaceports: About 20 licensed and federal launch and reentry sites.
- Connecticut DEEP boat launches: 117 state boat launches.
- Historic markers in the Atlanta region: 490 historic markers in the Atlanta region.
- Hampton Roads public water access sites: 208 boat ramps, kayak launches, fishing piers and beaches in the Hampton Roads region.
- Pennsylvania trailheads (DCNR): 318 trailheads (the same dataset's historical markers are already in pa-historical-markers).
- Wikidata names in other languages (optional names part): French, Spanish, German, Italian, Dutch, Japanese and Chinese names of the places with coordinates (people left out), keyed like the Wikidata places of the main packs ("wikidata:Q..."). In a separate, optional pack part ("names"), downloaded only when a user turns on other languages; the main packs stay as they are. Data only.
- NTAD North American Rail Network lines (named subdivisions, active and abandoned): Railway segments that carry a subdivision name ("Northern Subdivision"), active and abandoned (type abandoned railway). Segments with only an owner code are left out (no name).
- NTAD Amtrak routes: The 49 named Amtrak routes ("Vermonter", "Empire Builder") as lines.
- USGS National Hydrography Dataset named rivers and streams (medium resolution): Only the stream segments named in GNIS (a GNIS id), at medium resolution (1:100,000), so a picture beside a river gets the river's line, not only GNIS's point (about 15,000 segments in Vermont).
- USGS National Hydrography Dataset named lakes, ponds and reservoirs (medium resolution): Only the waterbodies named in GNIS, as areas (about 2,250 in Vermont), so "on Lake Bomoseen" is known by its shore.
- NRCan National Road Network: street names and address ranges: Road segments with their complete street names and the house numbers on each side, for every province and territory (6 MB for PEI to 237 MB for Ontario): "at about 120 Main Street" in Canada, and the address ranges the build-time geocoder falls back on when an address has no point.
- CanVec named lakes, ponds and rivers (areas, 1:50,000): Named waterbodies (lakes, ponds, wide rivers) from CanVec's 1:50,000 hydrography, which carries the National Hydro Network's names (874 in PEI). Only the provinces and territories whose CanVec hydrography file is under 1 GB (6 MB for PEI to 944 MB for Manitoba); British Columbia, Ontario, Québec, Newfoundland and Labrador, the Northwest Territories and Nunavut (1.1 to 6.4 GB each) are left to the CGNDB's points.
- CanVec named rivers and streams (lines, 1:50,000): Named watercourses as lines (1,181 in PEI), in the four provinces and territories with the smallest CanVec files; the larger ones would double a download of up to 944 MB.
- NTAD National Transit Map stations (stations and terminals only): Only stations, terminals, transit centres and park-and-rides out of the 673,251 stops of every US agency's feed (about 25,000 by name, plus stops their feed marks as stations unless the name is a street corner), so street-corner bus stops do not flood the packs; it fills the agencies whose own GTFS feed has no open licence. A station that the GTFS layer of the same pack already has (the same name within 150 m) is removed at build time; OpenStreetMap's stations are in the core pack and are merged at lookup time (the same name within 50 m is one place).
- Wikidata filming locations: Places that films and series were filmed at (Wikidata's filming location, P915, asked the other way round), each with a note "filming location of A; B; C" (up to 8 titles). The note is data only, never spoken; the point has the place's own Wikidata key, so a lookup joins it to the place.
- Who's On First neighbourhoods (open-source records only): Current neighbourhoods, macrohoods, microhoods, boroughs and campuses of the US and Canada (the admin bundles: 886 MB and 184 MB compressed), as points, kept only when the record's geometry source (src:geom) is open: Mapzen and Who's On First's own work (CC0), Mesoshapes, Natural Earth, the US Census, Quattroshapes, Zetashapes, GeoNames, GeoPlanet, Statistics Canada. OpenStreetMap-derived records (ODbL) and unknown sources are left out. Who's On First's venue bundles are no longer published.
- NCES public schools closed since 2015 (EDGE yearly files): The public schools of the yearly EDGE geocode files (2015-16 to 2024-25, about 30 MB each) that are no longer in the newest year, with their years: a closed or renamed school keeps its dates. The older CCD years (before 2015-16) have no geocode file of this kind on the EDGE site.
- New York City honorary street names and renamings (City Map alterations): About 1,100 honorary co-names and renamings of streets, corners, intersections and plazas, each with the date its local law took effect. Class variant: found by name search and never said as a place's name (the street keeps its official name in the lookup).
- Houston historic sites and landmarks: 422 designated landmarks and protected sites. Only the site's name and designation are asked for (the layer also holds owners' names and phone numbers, which are never fetched).
- Houston historic districts (City of Houston): 23 city historic districts, each from its establishment date.
- Fort Worth libraries and community centres: Libraries and community centres of Fort Worth.
- Fort Worth police stations: Police stations of Fort Worth.
- Fort Worth fire stations: The 45 fire stations of Fort Worth, named by number.
- Fort Worth parks: 314 parks, each from the year it was acquired or opened.
- City of Miami landmarks: About 700 named landmarks (parks, schools, hospitals, hotels, consulates, stations, marinas, beaches), typed by their category. Apartments, residential communities, retirement and nursing homes and ramps are left out.
- Sacramento County food facilities (inspections): Restaurants, bars, markets, bakeries and farmers' markets, one place per facility from its earliest to its latest inspection in the layer. Mobile food facilities, commissaries and satellite and school meal programmes are left out.
- Saint Paul historic sites: 115 designated historic sites, each from its year of designation.
- Tampa historic landmarks (local and national): About 140 local and national historic landmarks, each from the date of its ordinance.
- Arlington (Texas) hotels (inspections): Hotels and motels, one place each from its earliest to its latest inspection.
- Seattle historic and special review districts: Pioneer Square, the International Special Review District, Pike Place Market and the other historic districts.
- Seattle active business licence tax certificates (located by address): About 40,000 Seattle businesses (corporations, multi-member companies, partnerships, non-profits; sole proprietors and single-member companies are left out, as they are often a person at home), located by the offline geocoder against the National Address Database (an address that is a home is put on its street), typed by NAICS code. Only the trade name is kept, never the legal (often personal) name, owner or phone.
- Winnipeg business licences (personal sales and services): Licensed second-hand dealers, pawn shops, personal service shops and the like (about 2,700 licences since 2010), one place each from its first to its last licence. Rooming-house and residential licences (homes) are left out.
- City of Oakland public art collection: About 90 permanent public artworks (the artist's name is not kept).
